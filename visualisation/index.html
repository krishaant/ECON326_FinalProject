Air quality and income inequality

What am I looking at?

Each column is a Canadian city (a Census Metropolitan Area, or CMA, Statistics Canada's definition of a city and its surrounding commuter area).

Height = air pollution. AQHI (Air Quality Health Index) is Canada's official 1 to 10+ scale for how polluted the air is. Taller column means worse average air quality in 2024.

Color = income inequality. The Gini coefficient measures how evenly income is spread across a population, from 0 (everyone earns the same) to 1 (one person has all the income). Redder means more inequality in that city.

The small spikes are individual air quality monitoring stations, not cities.

The finding: cities with worse air quality tend to have higher income inequality, and the effect is strongest in lower-income cities.

Data: Environment and Climate Change Canada, Statistics Canada 2021 Census, Environics Analytics 2024
